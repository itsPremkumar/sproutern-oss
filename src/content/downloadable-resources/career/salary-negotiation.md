## 💰 1. Understanding Compensation

### Total Compensation Components

| Component            | Description                        | Negotiable?  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Base Salary**      | Fixed monthly/annual pay           | ✅ Yes       |
| **Bonus**            | Performance-based (10-30% typical) | ✅ Yes       |
| **Stock/ESOP**       | Equity in the company              | ✅ Yes       |
| **Signing Bonus**    | One-time joining bonus             | ✅ Yes       |
| **Relocation**       | Moving expenses                    | ✅ Yes       |
| **PF/Gratuity**      | Retirement benefits                | ⚠️ Sometimes |
| **Health Insurance** | Medical coverage                   | ⚠️ Sometimes |
| **Leave Policy**     | PTO, sick leave                    | ⚠️ Sometimes |
| **Learning Budget**  | Courses, conferences               | ✅ Yes       |
| **Remote Work**      | WFH flexibility                    | ✅ Yes       |

### CTC vs In-Hand Salary

**CTC (Cost to Company)** includes everything: base + bonuses + benefits + employer PF

**In-Hand Salary** is what you actually receive monthly

**Quick Formula:**

```
In-Hand ≈ CTC × 0.65 to 0.75 (depending on tax slab and components)
```

## 🔍 2. Research Phase

### Step 1: Know Your Market Value

**Resources:**

- **Glassdoor**: Salary ranges by role, company, location
- **LinkedIn Salary**: Uses LinkedIn data
- **Levels.fyi**: Best for tech companies (accurate equity data)
- **AmbitionBox**: India-specific salary data
- **PayScale**: Detailed compensation reports

### Step 2: Create a Salary Range

**Formula:**

```
Target Salary = Current Salary × 1.20 to 1.40 (20-40% hike)

OR for freshers:
Target = Industry Average for Role + Location + Company Tier
```

**Three numbers to know:**

1. **Walk-away number**: Minimum you'll accept
2. **Target number**: What you're aiming for
3. **Aspiration number**: Best-case scenario

**Example:**

- Walk-away: ₹12 LPA
- Target: ₹15 LPA
- Aspiration: ₹18 LPA

### Step 3: Assess Your Leverage

**High leverage if:**

- ✅ You have competing offers
- ✅ You have rare skills (ML, blockchain, etc.)
- ✅ The company reached out to you
- ✅ You're currently employed
- ✅ You have strong referrals

**Low leverage if:**

- ❌ You're unemployed for 6+ months
- ❌ No other offers
- ❌ Very junior with common skills
- ❌ You applied without referral

## ⏰ 3. Timing Your Negotiation

### The Golden Rule

> **Never share a number first.** Let them anchor.

### Timeline

| Stage                  | What Happens             | What You Say                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Application**        | Salary expectation field | Leave blank or write "Negotiable"                                                                 |
| **Initial Screening**  | HR asks expectations     | "I'm focused on finding the right role. Happy to discuss compensation once we're aligned on fit." |
| **During Interviews**  | They may push for number | "I'd prefer to learn more about the role first. What's the budgeted range?"                       |
| **After Verbal Offer** | They make first offer    | "Thank you! I'm excited. Let me review the complete package."                                     |
| **Negotiation Window** | 24-72 hours after offer  | This is when you negotiate                                                                        |
| **Written Offer**      | Final terms              | Last chance for small adjustments                                                                 |

### When NOT to Negotiate

- Very clearly stated "non-negotiable" government pay bands
- When you've already accepted verbally without conditions
- After you've signed the offer letter

## 🗣️ 4. Negotiation Scripts

### Script 1: Responding to "What are your salary expectations?"

**Early in process:**

```
"At this stage, I'm most focused on finding the right fit.
I'm confident we can find a number that works for both of us
once we've established mutual interest. What does the
budgeted range for this role look like?"
```

**If they insist:**

```
"Based on my research and experience, I'm targeting
the ₹X to ₹Y range, but I'm flexible depending on
the total compensation and growth opportunity."
```

### Script 2: Receiving the Initial Offer

```
"Thank you so much for the offer! I'm really excited about
the opportunity to join [Company] and work on [specific project].

I'd like to take a day to review the complete package.
Could you send me the detailed breakdown in writing?"
```

### Script 3: Counter-Offer Conversation

```
"I've thought about this carefully, and I'm very enthusiastic
about the role. However, based on my research on market rates
for similar positions and the value I believe I can bring—
particularly my experience with [specific skill]—I was hoping
we could explore a base salary closer to ₹X.

Is there flexibility in the compensation?"
```

### Script 4: When They Say "This is our best offer"

```
"I understand there may be constraints on base salary.
Are there other components we could discuss? For example:
- A signing bonus to bridge the gap?
- A performance bonus with clear metrics?
- Additional equity or ESOP?
- A salary review in 6 months tied to performance?"
```

### Script 5: Accepting the Offer

```
"Thank you for working with me on this. I'm happy to accept
the offer of ₹X base with [other terms]. I'm looking forward
to contributing to [Company] and starting on [date].

Could you please send the revised offer letter?"
```

## 🔄 5. Counter-Offer Strategies

### The Counter-Offer Formula

```
Counter = Initial Offer + ( Aspiration - Initial Offer ) × 0.5 to 0.7
```

**Example:**

- Initial offer: ₹14 LPA
- Your aspiration: ₹18 LPA
- Counter: ₹14 + (₹18 - ₹14) × 0.6 = ₹16.4 LPA → Ask for ₹17 LPA

### Strategy Matrix

| Situation                    | Strategy                                        |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Offer is below walk-away** | Express concern, ask if there's flexibility     |
| **Offer is at walk-away**    | Counter at target, negotiate hard on benefits   |
| **Offer is at target**       | Counter at aspiration, likely settle in between |
| **Offer exceeds aspiration** | Double-check equity/bonus, accept gracefully    |

### Using Competing Offers

**If you have another offer:**

```
"I should be transparent—I've received another offer for ₹X.
I prefer [Company] because of [specific reason], but the
compensation gap is significant. Is there any way to
close that difference?"
```

**If you don't have another offer:** Imply demand without lying:

```
"I'm in active conversations with a few other companies,
and I want to make a decision soon. [Company] is my
top choice, but I need the compensation to reflect that."
```

## 🎁 6. Negotiating Benefits

### When Base Salary is Fixed

If they can't move on base, negotiate these:

| Benefit             | How to Ask                                                                           |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Signing Bonus**   | "Would a one-time signing bonus be possible to bridge the gap?"                      |
| **Early Review**    | "Can we schedule a salary review in 6 months with a 15% increase if I meet targets?" |
| **Stock Options**   | "Could we increase the equity component?"                                            |
| **Flexible Hours**  | "Would 2-3 days of remote work per week be possible?"                                |
| **Learning Budget** | "Is there a budget for courses and conferences?"                                     |
| **Notice Period**   | "Can I start after a 4-week notice instead of 8?"                                    |
| **Title**           | "Would 'Senior' title be possible? It impacts future opportunities."                 |
| **Relocation**      | "Can the company cover relocation expenses?"                                         |

### Prioritization Framework

Rate each benefit on: Impact (1-10) × Importance to you (1-10)

| Benefit              | Impact | Your Importance | Score |
| -------------------- | ------ | --------------- | ----- |
| Base salary +₹3L     | 8      | 9               | 72 ⭐ |
| Remote 3 days/week   | 7      | 10              | 70 ⭐ |
| Signing bonus ₹2L    | 5      | 6               | 30    |
| Learning budget ₹50K | 4      | 7               | 28    |

Focus on top 2-3 priorities.

## ❌ 7. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Sharing Your Current Salary

**Why it's bad:** Anchors negotiation to your current pay, not market value.

**What to say:**

```
"I prefer to focus on the value I'll bring to this role
rather than my current compensation. What matters is
whether we can find a number that works for both of us."
```

### Mistake 2: Accepting Immediately

**Why it's bad:** You leave money on the table. Companies expect negotiation.

**What to do:** Always ask for 24-48 hours to review.

### Mistake 3: Only Negotiating Base Salary

**Why it's bad:** Total comp matters. Stock options, bonuses, and benefits add up.

**What to do:** Negotiate the full package.

### Mistake 4: Being Aggressive or Demanding

**Why it's bad:** Damages relationship before you even start.

**What to do:** Be firm but collaborative. "I'm excited about us finding something that works."

### Mistake 5: Not Having a Walk-Away Number

**Why it's bad:** You may accept something you regret.

**What to do:** Know your minimum. Be willing to walk away.

### Mistake 6: Lying About Competing Offers

**Why it's bad:** If caught, offer gets rescinded and your reputation is damaged.

**What to do:** Only mention offers you actually have.

## 📧 8. Email Templates

### Template 1: Requesting Time to Review

**Subject:** Re: Offer for [Role] Position

```
Dear [Recruiter Name],

Thank you so much for the offer to join [Company] as a [Role].
I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity, especially
[specific reason].

I'd like to review the complete compensation details carefully.
Could you share a written breakdown, and give me a couple of
days to consider?

I'll get back to you by [date].

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Template 2: Counter-Offer Email

**Subject:** Re: Offer for [Role] Position - Compensation Discussion

```
Dear [Recruiter Name],

Thank you again for extending this offer. After careful
consideration, I'd like to discuss the compensation package.

Based on my research of market rates for similar roles
and my [X years] of experience in [skill], I was hoping
for a base salary closer to ₹[X] LPA.

I'm confident this aligns with the value I'll bring—
particularly in [specific area]. Is there flexibility
to adjust the package?

I'm very enthusiastic about joining [Company] and believe
we can find a mutually beneficial arrangement.

Looking forward to discussing this.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Template 3: Accepting the Offer

**Subject:** Acceptance - [Role] Position

```
Dear [Recruiter Name],

Thank you for the updated offer. I'm delighted to accept
the position of [Role] at [Company] with a compensation
of ₹[X] LPA as discussed.

I'm excited to contribute to [specific team/project] and
look forward to starting on [date].

Please let me know the next steps regarding documentation
and onboarding.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Template 4: Declining an Offer Professionally

**Subject:** Re: Offer for [Role] Position

```
Dear [Recruiter Name],

Thank you for the offer to join [Company] as [Role].
After careful thought, I've decided to pursue another
opportunity that better aligns with my career goals
at this time.

I genuinely appreciate the time you and the team invested
in the interview process. [Company] is doing impressive
work, and I hope our paths cross again in the future.

Best wishes,
[Your Name]
```

## 🎭 9. Special Situations

### Situation: First Job / Fresher

**Reality:** Less leverage, but still negotiate!

**What to negotiate:**

- If multiple offers, use them
- Learning opportunities and mentorship
- Early salary review (6 months)
- Team/project preference
- Training programs

**Script:**

```
"I understand this is an entry-level role. I'm wondering
if there's room for a performance-based review at 6 months?
If I exceed expectations, could we revisit compensation?"
```

### Situation: Internal Promotion

**Script:**

```
"I'm excited about this new opportunity. Given the
increase in responsibilities—including [specific new duties]—
I'd like to discuss how the compensation reflects this change.

Based on market data for [role title], I believe ₹[X]
would be appropriate."
```

### Situation: Startup vs. Corporate

**Startup considerations:**

- Lower base, higher equity
- Ask about vesting schedule (typically 4 years, 1-year cliff)
- Get equity details in writing
- Understand liquidation preferences

**Questions to ask:**

- What's the current valuation?
- What strike price would my options be at?
- What's the runway? When is the next funding round?

### Situation: Layoff / Unemployment

**Leverage is lower, but:**

- Never show desperation
- Focus on value you bring
- Have a walk-away number
- Negotiate timeline or signing bonus instead

## 🎯 10. Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Low Initial Offer

**Offer:** ₹8 LPA for a role you expected ₹12 LPA

**Response:**

```
"Thank you for the offer. I'm genuinely interested in [Company],
but this is significantly below what I was expecting based on
market research for this role. I was targeting the ₹12-14 LPA range.

Is there room to discuss this, or should I understand this
as the final number?"
```

### Scenario 2: "This is our final offer"

**Response:**

```
"I understand. Before we finalize, could we explore other
components? Perhaps a signing bonus, or a structured review
with guaranteed increase in 6 months?

I want to make this work because I'm excited about [Company]."
```

### Scenario 3: HR Pressures You for Quick Decision

**Response:**

```
"I appreciate your timeline. This is a significant decision
for me, and I want to make sure I'm fully committed when I say yes.

Could I have until [specific date]? I'll give you a
definitive answer then."
```

### Scenario 4: They Ask About Current Salary

**Response:**

```
"I prefer not to anchor on my current salary, as it doesn't
fully reflect market rates for this role and level.

What's more relevant is the value I'll bring to [Company]
and what a fair market rate would be for this position."
```

## 📋 Negotiation Checklist

### Before Negotiation

- [ ] Researched market salary range
- [ ] Defined walk-away, target, and aspiration numbers
- [ ] Identified 2-3 non-salary priorities
- [ ] Practiced scripts out loud
- [ ] Prepared evidence of your value

### During Negotiation

- [ ] Let them name the first number
- [ ] Responded with enthusiasm, then paused
- [ ] Asked for full package details in writing
- [ ] Took 24-48 hours to consider
- [ ] Made counter-offer with rationale

### After Negotiation

- [ ] Got revised offer in writing
- [ ] Reviewed all terms carefully
- [ ] Sent formal acceptance email
- [ ] Thanked everyone involved
- [ ] Kept relationship positive (you'll work with these people!)

---

**Remember:** Negotiation is expected. Companies budget for it. The worst they can say is "no." Ask
confidently, listen carefully, and find the win-win.

Good luck! 🚀
