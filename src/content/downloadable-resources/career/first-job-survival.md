## 🎯 Before Day 1

### Documentation to Prepare

- [ ] Government ID (Aadhaar, PAN, Passport)
- [ ] Educational certificates (originals + copies)
- [ ] Previous employment documents (if any)
- [ ] Bank account details
- [ ] Passport-size photos
- [ ] Address proof
- [ ] Emergency contact information

### Research Your Company

- [ ] Re-read the job description
- [ ] Understand company's products/services
- [ ] Know your team and manager's names
- [ ] Read recent news about the company
- [ ] Familiarize yourself with company values
- [ ] Check LinkedIn profiles of teammates

### Logistics

- [ ] Confirm start date, time, and location
- [ ] Plan your commute (do a trial run)
- [ ] Prepare appropriate work attire
- [ ] Set up professional voicemail
- [ ] Install any required apps (Slack, Teams, etc.)

### Mindset Preparation

- **You were hired for a reason** – They believe in you
- **Imposter syndrome is normal** – Everyone feels it initially
- **It's okay not to know everything** – You're expected to learn
- **First impressions matter** – Be punctual, positive, professional

## 📅 Week 1: Orientation

### Day 1 Goals

1. **Arrive 15 minutes early**
2. **Listen more than you talk**
3. **Remember names** (take notes if needed)
4. **Ask clarifying questions**
5. **Say yes to lunch invitations**

### What to Expect

| Activity                   | What to Do                               |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **HR Onboarding**          | Take notes, ask questions about benefits |
| **IT Setup**               | Get laptop, credentials, access          |
| **Team Introduction**      | Smile, handshake, brief intro            |
| **Office Tour**            | Note important locations                 |
| **First 1:1 with Manager** | Clarify expectations                     |

### Key Questions to Ask Your Manager

```markdown
1. What does success look like in this role after 30/60/90 days?
2. How do you prefer to communicate? (Slack, email, in-person)
3. How often should we have 1:1s?
4. Who are the key people I should build relationships with?
5. What's the most important project I should understand first?
6. Is there anything you wish you knew when you started here?
```

### Week 1 Survival Tips

- **Take extensive notes** – You won't remember everything
- **Over-communicate** – Confirm tasks via message/email
- **Don't be afraid to ask** – "Stupid" questions now save embarrassment later
- **Stay humble** – Even if you see issues, observe first
- **Learn the tools** – Master Slack/Teams, Jira, Git, internal systems

## 📚 Month 1: Learning Mode

### The 70-20-10 Learning Model

- **70%**: Learning by doing (hands-on tasks)
- **20%**: Learning from others (mentors, colleagues)
- **10%**: Formal training (courses, documentation)

### Build Your Knowledge Base

**Create a personal wiki with:**

- Team structure and roles
- Important contacts and their expertise
- Key processes and workflows
- Acronym glossary
- Meeting notes and action items
- Links to important documents

### Shadow and Observe

Ask to shadow team members in:

- Sprint planning meetings
- Code reviews
- Client calls
- Cross-team collaborations

### Quick Wins

Look for small ways to add value early:

- Fix a small bug
- Update outdated documentation
- Improve a testing process
- Automate a repetitive task
- Share a useful resource with the team

### Building Relationships

**Key people to connect with:**

| Role               | Why Important                |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Direct Manager     | Your advocate and guide      |
| Buddy/Mentor       | Go-to for daily questions    |
| Peers              | Day-to-day collaboration     |
| Skip-level Manager | Career visibility            |
| HR Contact         | Benefits, policies, concerns |
| IT Support         | Technical issues             |
| Senior Engineers   | Technical guidance           |

**How to connect:**

- Request 15-30 minute "intro coffee chats"
- Ask about their role and career journey
- Find common interests
- Follow up on conversations

## 🚀 Month 2-3: Contributing

### Transition from Learning to Doing

| Phase     | Focus                            |
| --------- | -------------------------------- |
| Week 1-2  | Listen and observe               |
| Week 3-4  | Ask questions and clarify        |
| Week 5-8  | Start contributing with guidance |
| Week 9-12 | Own small projects independently |

### Taking on Your First Project

**Before starting:**

1. Clarify the scope and expectations
2. Identify stakeholders
3. Understand the timeline
4. Know who to escalate to

**During:**

1. Break it into smaller tasks
2. Update progress regularly
3. Ask for help early (not when stuck for hours)
4. Document your work

**After:**

1. Get feedback
2. Do a mini-retrospective
3. Share learnings with the team

### Feedback Loop

**Seek feedback proactively:**

```markdown
"Hey [Manager], I wanted to check in on how I'm doing. Is there anything specific I should focus on
or improve? Any blind spots I should be aware of?"
```

**When receiving feedback:**

- Listen without being defensive
- Ask clarifying questions
- Thank them genuinely
- Create an action plan
- Follow up on progress

## 💬 Communication Skills

### Email Etiquette

**Do:**

- Clear subject line: "Action Required: API Review by Friday"
- State purpose in first line
- Keep it concise (5 sentences max)
- Use bullet points for clarity
- Include clear call-to-action
- Proofread before sending

**Don't:**

- Reply-all unnecessarily
- Write essays
- Use text speak (u, pls, thx)
- CC your manager to escalate passively
- Send emotional emails

**Email Template:**

```
Subject: [Action/FYI/Question] Brief Description

Hi [Name],

[1 sentence context]

[Body - key points in bullets]
- Point 1
- Point 2

[Clear ask/next step]

Thanks,
[Your Name]
```

### Meeting Etiquette

**Before:**

- Review agenda
- Prepare your input
- Join on time (or 1 min early)

**During:**

- Mute when not speaking
- Camera on (when possible)
- Take notes
- Ask questions at appropriate times
- Summarize action items at the end

**After:**

- Send follow-up with notes/action items
- Complete your action items on time

### Slack/Teams Communication

| Situation         | Best Practice        |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Quick question    | DM the right person  |
| Team announcement | Use team channel     |
| Urgent issue      | DM + tag in channel  |
| Long discussion   | Move to call/meeting |
| Sensitive topic   | Private conversation |

**Status etiquette:**

- 🟢 Available – You're free
- 🟡 Away – Be back soon
- 🔴 DND – Focus time, don't interrupt
- 📅 In a meeting – Check calendar

## 👔 Managing Your Manager

### Understanding Your Manager's Style

Ask yourself:

- How do they prefer to communicate?
- What are their priorities and pressures?
- How do they define success?
- What frustrates them?

### How to Have Effective 1:1s

**Prepare an agenda:**

```markdown
## 1:1 Agenda - [Date]

### Updates

- Completed X, Y, Z this week
- Currently working on A

### Blockers

- Need access to B system
- Waiting on feedback from C

### Discussion

- Career question about growth path
- Idea about improving process D

### Feedback Request

- How am I doing on project E?
```

### Communicating Problems

**Don't:** "This is broken. What should I do?"

**Do:** "I found an issue with X. Here's what I've tried: A, B, C. I think the best option is B
because [reason]. Does that approach make sense?"

### Managing Up Effectively

1. **Understand their goals** – Help them look good
2. **Be reliable** – Meet deadlines, update proactively
3. **Don't surprise them** – Escalate bad news early
4. **Propose solutions** – Don't just bring problems
5. **Respect their time** – Batch non-urgent questions
6. **Give them credit** – Publicly, generously

## 🎭 Navigating Office Politics

### The Reality

Office politics exists everywhere. It's not about being manipulative—it's about being smart.

### Do's

- **Build genuine relationships** – Get to know people as humans
- **Understand the org chart** – Both formal and informal
- **Stay neutral** – Avoid taking sides in conflicts
- **Give credit** – Acknowledge others' contributions
- **Document everything** – Especially decisions and agreements
- **Be consistently reliable** – Trust is built over time

### Don'ts

- **Don't gossip** – Assume everything gets back to the person
- **Don't burn bridges** – Industries are smaller than you think
- **Don't complain publicly** – It affects your reputation
- **Don't take sides** – In team conflicts, stay professional
- **Don't overpromise** – Under-promise, over-deliver

### When You Disagree

**With peers:**

```
"I see your point about X. I was thinking about it
differently because of [reason]. Can we discuss the
trade-offs?"
```

**With your manager:**

```
"I understand the direction. I want to share a concern:
[issue]. I might be missing context—can you help me
understand why this approach is best?"
```

**When to escalate:**

- Safety or ethical concerns
- Harassment or discrimination
- Clear policy violations
- After trying to resolve directly first

## ❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Not Asking Questions

**Fear:** "They'll think I'm stupid" **Reality:** Not asking leads to bigger mistakes **Fix:** "Can
I clarify [X]? I want to make sure I understand correctly."

### Mistake 2: Working in Isolation

**Fear:** "I don't want to bother people" **Reality:** Collaboration is expected **Fix:** Regular
check-ins, share progress, ask for quick reviews

### Mistake 3: Saying Yes to Everything

**Fear:** "I want to impress everyone" **Reality:** You'll burn out and under-deliver **Fix:** "I'd
love to help. Let me check my current priorities and get back to you."

### Mistake 4: Not Taking Notes

**Reality:** You _will_ forget. Write everything down. **Fix:** Keep a daily log of tasks,
learnings, and decisions

### Mistake 5: Comparing Yourself to Others

**Reality:** You see their output, not their journey **Fix:** Focus on your own growth
week-over-week

### Mistake 6: Not Taking Breaks

**Reality:** Productivity drops without rest **Fix:** Take lunch breaks, use PTO, set boundaries

### Mistake 7: Avoiding Feedback

**Reality:** Feedback is a gift—it accelerates growth **Fix:** Request feedback regularly, act on it
visibly

## 📈 Growth Mindset

### Learning Continuously

**Daily habits:**

- Read industry news (15 min)
- Learn one new thing (could be small)
- Reflect on what went well/poorly

**Weekly habits:**

- Attend one meeting outside your team
- Have one coffee chat
- Update your personal wiki

**Monthly habits:**

- Complete a course module
- Write about what you learned (blog, notes)
- Set learning goals for next month

### Building Your Personal Brand

- **Be known for something** – Deep expertise > shallow breadth
- **Share your work** – Demos, docs, presentations
- **Help others** – Answer questions, mentor juniors
- **Volunteer for visibility** – Cross-team projects, all-hands

### Tracking Your Wins

Keep a "brag document" with:

- Projects completed
- Problems solved
- Positive feedback received
- Skills learned
- Metrics improved

**Why:** Essential for performance reviews and resume updates

## ⚖️ Work-Life Balance

### Setting Boundaries

- **Work hours:** Define when you start/end
- **Communication:** Set expectations on response times
- **Weekends/PTO:** Truly disconnect when off

**How to say no:**

```
"I can help with this, but it would mean pushing back [other task].
Which should I prioritize?"
```

### Signs of Burnout

- Constant exhaustion
- Cynicism about work
- Decreased productivity
- Physical symptoms (headaches, sleep issues)
- Dreading Mondays

**What to do:**

1. Talk to your manager
2. Take PTO
3. Re-evaluate workload
4. Seek support (friends, mentors, professionals)

### Healthy Habits

- Take lunch away from desk
- Use breaks (Pomodoro technique)
- Exercise regularly
- Have hobbies outside work
- Maintain relationships outside work
