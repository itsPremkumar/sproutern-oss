## 🗣️ 1. Communication Skills

### The 4 Components of Communication

| Component      | Description          | Tips                               |
| -------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Verbal**     | What you say         | Be clear, concise, structured      |
| **Non-verbal** | Body language        | Maintain eye contact, open posture |
| **Written**    | Emails, docs         | Professional, error-free           |
| **Listening**  | Understanding others | Active listening, ask questions    |

### Active Listening

**LISTEN Framework:**

- **L**ook at the speaker
- **I**nquire with questions
- **S**ummarize what you heard
- **T**ake notes
- **E**mpathize with their perspective
- **N**od and show engagement

**Phrases for Active Listening:**

```
"If I understand correctly, you're saying..."
"Can you tell me more about..."
"What I'm hearing is..."
"How did that make you feel?"
```

### Clear Communication

**The PREP Method:**

- **P**oint – State your main point first
- **R**eason – Explain why
- **E**xample – Give a concrete example
- **P**oint – Restate your main point

**Example:**

```
"I think we should delay the launch. (Point)
Our testing has revealed 3 critical bugs. (Reason)
For example, the payment flow fails on mobile. (Example)
So I recommend we delay by one week to fix these. (Point)"
```

### Tailoring Communication

| Audience                       | Adjust                                    |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Executives**                 | High-level, focus on impact, be brief     |
| **Technical peers**            | Include details, use jargon appropriately |
| **Non-technical stakeholders** | Avoid jargon, use analogies               |
| **Clients**                    | Focus on their needs and benefits         |

## 💭 2. Emotional Intelligence (EQ)

### The 5 Components (Goleman Model)

| Component           | Description            | How to Develop                       |
| ------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Self-Awareness**  | Knowing your emotions  | Reflect, journal, ask for feedback   |
| **Self-Regulation** | Managing your emotions | Pause before reacting, count to 10   |
| **Motivation**      | Internal drive         | Set goals, find purpose in work      |
| **Empathy**         | Understanding others   | Listen deeply, consider perspectives |
| **Social Skills**   | Managing relationships | Build rapport, resolve conflicts     |

### Self-Awareness Exercises

**Daily Reflection Questions:**

1. What emotion am I feeling right now?
2. What triggered this emotion?
3. How is this affecting my behavior?
4. What would a calmer response look like?

**Journaling Prompts:**

- What situation triggered a strong reaction today?
- What's a pattern I notice in my emotional responses?
- What feedback have I received about my behavior?

### Managing Difficult Emotions at Work

**When Angry:**

```
1. Pause – Don't react immediately
2. Breathe – 3 deep breaths
3. Describe – "I'm feeling frustrated because..."
4. Request – "I need a few minutes" OR "Can we discuss this later?"
```

**When Anxious:**

```
1. Ground yourself – Feel your feet on the floor
2. Name it – "I'm feeling anxious about the presentation"
3. Challenge – "What's the worst that could happen? Is it likely?"
4. Prepare – Take one small action to feel more prepared
```

### Developing Empathy

**Perspective-Taking Exercise:** Before judging someone's action, ask:

- What might they be going through?
- What pressures are they facing?
- How would I feel in their situation?
- What's a charitable interpretation of their behavior?

## 🤝 3. Teamwork & Collaboration

### Stages of Team Development (Tuckman's Model)

| Stage          | Characteristics                  | Your Role                       |
| -------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Forming**    | Polite, cautious, learning       | Be friendly, ask questions      |
| **Storming**   | Conflict, power struggles        | Stay calm, listen, compromise   |
| **Norming**    | Trust building, agreements       | Contribute ideas, follow norms  |
| **Performing** | High productivity, collaboration | Take initiative, support others |

### Being a Great Team Member

**Do:**

- Meet commitments and deadlines
- Communicate proactively
- Help others when you can
- Celebrate team wins
- Address conflicts directly but kindly
- Give credit generously

**Don't:**

- Gossip about teammates
- Take credit for others' work
- Stay silent when you disagree
- Miss meetings without notice
- Create silos

### Virtual Collaboration Tips

| Challenge            | Solution                                |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Miscommunication     | Over-communicate, confirm understanding |
| Feeling disconnected | Regular video calls, casual check-ins   |
| Timezone differences | Clear async communication, shared docs  |
| Tech issues          | Have backup communication channels      |

## 👑 4. Leadership Skills

### Leadership vs. Management

| Leadership               | Management                 |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Vision and direction     | Planning and organizing    |
| Inspiring and motivating | Controlling and monitoring |
| Innovation and change    | Stability and consistency  |
| People development       | Task execution             |

### Leading Without Authority

You don't need a title to lead:

1. **Take initiative** – Volunteer for problems no one is solving
2. **Be the example** – Model the behavior you want to see
3. **Help others succeed** – Mentor, share knowledge
4. **Build consensus** – Bring people together around ideas
5. **Own outcomes** – Take responsibility, not blame

### Situational Leadership

Adapt your style based on the team member's competence and commitment:

| Situation                        | Style      | Approach                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| New/learning                     | Directing  | Give clear instructions, close supervision |
| Some competence, low confidence  | Coaching   | Explain, encourage, guide                  |
| Competent, variable commitment   | Supporting | Listen, facilitate, praise                 |
| High competence, high commitment | Delegating | Trust, empower, step back                  |

### Developing Others

**GROW Coaching Model:**

- **G**oal – What do you want to achieve?
- **R**eality – Where are you now?
- **O**ptions – What could you do?
- **W**ill – What will you do?

**Sample Coaching Questions:**

```
Goal: "What would success look like for you?"
Reality: "What's happening right now?"
Options: "What are three different approaches you could take?"
Will: "Which option feels right? What's your next step?"
```

## ⏰ 5. Time Management

### The Eisenhower Matrix

```
             URGENT            NOT URGENT
           ─────────────────────────────────
 IMPORTANT │   DO FIRST    │    SCHEDULE    │
           │  Crises       │  Planning      │
           │  Deadlines    │  Learning      │
           ─────────────────────────────────
           │   DELEGATE    │    ELIMINATE   │
 NOT       │  Interruptions│  Time wasters  │
 IMPORTANT │  Some emails  │  Busy work     │
           ─────────────────────────────────
```

### Time Blocking

**Sample Day Structure:**

```
7:00-8:00   - Morning routine
8:00-8:30   - Daily planning, inbox
8:30-11:00  - Deep work block 1 (most important task)
11:00-11:30 - Email/Slack catchup
11:30-12:30 - Meetings
12:30-1:30  - Lunch break (away from desk!)
1:30-3:00   - Deep work block 2
3:00-3:30   - Coffee break, walk
3:30-5:00   - Collaborative work, meetings
5:00-5:30   - EOD planning, shutdown
```

### Productivity Techniques

**Pomodoro Technique:**

1. Work for 25 minutes
2. Take 5-minute break
3. After 4 pomodoros, take 15-30 minute break

**Two-Minute Rule:** If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it now.

**Eat the Frog:** Do your hardest/most important task first thing in the morning.

### Saying No Gracefully

```
"I appreciate you thinking of me. Unfortunately, I can't
take this on right now as I'm committed to [X].
Could we revisit this next month/Can I suggest [alternative]?"
```

```
"I'd love to help, but my plate is full. Let me check
with [manager] about priorities and get back to you."
```

## 🧩 6. Problem Solving

### The 5 Whys Technique

Keep asking "Why?" to get to the root cause.

**Example:**

- Problem: The report was late
- Why? → The data wasn't ready
- Why? → The query failed
- Why? → The database was down
- Why? → The server ran out of memory
- Why? → We didn't have monitoring alerts
- **Root cause:** Lack of monitoring

### Structured Problem Solving

**IDEAL Framework:**

1. **I**dentify the problem
2. **D**efine the problem clearly
3. **E**xplore possible solutions
4. **A**ct on the best solution
5. **L**ook back and learn

### Creative Problem Solving

**Brainstorming Rules:**

- Quantity over quality initially
- No criticism during ideation
- Build on others' ideas
- Encourage wild ideas

**Techniques:**

- **Reverse brainstorming:** "How could we make this problem worse?"
- **SCAMPER:** Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Rearrange
- **Six Thinking Hats:** Consider problem from different perspectives

## ⚔️ 7. Conflict Resolution

### Conflict Styles (Thomas-Kilmann Model)

| Style             | When to Use                              |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Avoiding**      | Issue is trivial, emotions are high      |
| **Accommodating** | Relationship matters more than issue     |
| **Competing**     | Quick decision needed, you're certain    |
| **Compromising**  | Time-limited, both parties have power    |
| **Collaborating** | Issue is important, relationship matters |

### The DESC Script for Conflict

- **D**escribe – State the situation factually
- **E**xpress – Share your feelings/concerns
- **S**pecify – Request specific change
- **C**onsequences – Explain positive outcome

**Example:**

```
"When deadlines are missed without notice (D),
I feel frustrated because it affects my planning (E).
Could you let me know by noon if there's a delay? (S)
That way we can adjust expectations together. (C)"
```

### Mediating Conflicts

**Steps:**

1. Meet individually first – Understand each perspective
2. Bring parties together – Neutral space
3. Set ground rules – Respectful conversation
4. Let each share perspective – No interruptions
5. Identify common ground – What do both want?
6. Brainstorm solutions – Together
7. Agree on action plan – Document it
8. Follow up – Check progress

## 💬 8. Giving & Receiving Feedback

### The SBI Model (Situation-Behavior-Impact)

**Structure:**

- **S**ituation – Where and when
- **B**ehavior – What you observed (not your interpretation)
- **I**mpact – How it affected you/the team

**Examples:**

**Constructive:**

```
"In yesterday's client call (S),
you interrupted the client twice (B).
I noticed they seemed frustrated and we lost their attention (I)."
```

**Positive:**

```
"In this morning's presentation (S),
you explained the technical concepts using great analogies (B).
The clients understood immediately and approved the proposal (I)."
```

### Feedback Sandwich (Use Sparingly)

```
1. Positive opening
2. Constructive feedback
3. Positive/encouraging close

"Your code quality has really improved. One thing to work on
is the documentation—adding comments would help the team.
I can see you're putting in effort, and that's great!"
```

### Receiving Feedback Gracefully

**Do:**

- Thank them for the feedback
- Listen without defending
- Ask clarifying questions
- Summarize to confirm understanding
- Take time to reflect
- Create an action plan

**Don't:**

- Argue or get defensive
- Make excuses
- Take it personally
- Ignore it completely

**Phrases:**

```
"Thank you for sharing that. Can you give me an example?"
"I hadn't realized that. I'll work on it."
"That's helpful. What would you suggest I do differently?"
```

## 🎤 9. Presentation Skills

### Structure Your Presentation

**Opening (10%):**

- Hook: Story, statistic, question
- Introduce yourself briefly
- State your objective

**Body (80%):**

- Maximum 3 main points
- Each point: Statement → Evidence → Transition

**Closing (10%):**

- Summarize key points
- Clear call to action
- Strong closing statement

### Slide Design Tips

**Do:**

- One idea per slide
- Minimal text (6 words per line, 6 lines max)
- Large fonts (24+ pt)
- High-contrast colors
- Relevant visuals

**Don't:**

- Read from slides
- Use bullet-point walls
- Include complex tables
- Use cheesy clip art
- Add distracting transitions

### Managing Nerves

**Before:**

- Practice out loud 5+ times
- Visualize success
- Do power poses
- Arrive early, check tech

**During:**

- Breathe deeply before starting
- Start with your hook (you've memorized this)
- Make eye contact with friendly faces
- Pause when nervous (it looks confident)
- It's okay to say "Let me think about that"

### Handling Q&A

```
1. Listen fully – Don't interrupt
2. Pause – Think before answering
3. Thank them – "Great question"
4. Repeat/clarify – "So you're asking about..."
4. Answer concisely – Don't ramble
5. Confirm – "Does that answer your question?"
```

If you don't know:

```
"I don't have that information right now, but I'll
find out and get back to you by [time]."
```

## 🌐 10. Networking Skills

### Building Genuine Connections

**It's not about collecting contacts—it's about building relationships.**

**The FORD Method (Conversation starters):**

- **F**amily – "Where are you from originally?"
- **O**ccupation – "What's the most interesting project you're working on?"
- **R**ecreation – "What do you do for fun outside work?"
- **D**reams – "What's on your bucket list?"

### Networking at Events

**Before:**

- Research attendees you want to meet
- Prepare your introduction
- Set a goal (e.g., 5 meaningful conversations)

**During:**

- Arrive early (easier to talk to people)
- Look for people standing alone
- Ask open-ended questions
- Listen more than you talk
- Exchange contact info naturally

**After:**

- Connect on LinkedIn within 24 hours
- Send personalized follow-up message
- Add notes about your conversation

### Following Up

**Template:**

```
Hi [Name],

It was great meeting you at [event] yesterday. I enjoyed
our conversation about [topic].

[Personalized comment or reference to something they shared]

I'd love to stay in touch. [Optional: specific suggestion
for future interaction]

Best,
[Your Name]
```

### Maintaining Your Network

- **Engage online:** Like, comment, share their content
- **Check in periodically:** "Thinking of you, hope you're well"
- **Give before asking:** Share articles, make introductions
- **Remember details:** Birthdays, milestones, interests

## 🔄 11. Adaptability & Resilience

### Embracing Change

**Mindset Shift:**

- Change is constant in modern workplaces
- Resistance costs more energy than adaptation
- Change often brings opportunity

**When facing change:**

1. Acknowledge your feelings (it's okay to feel uncertain)
2. Focus on what you can control
3. Look for the opportunity within the change
4. Seek information to reduce uncertainty
5. Take small steps to adapt

### Building Resilience

**The 7 Cs of Resilience:**

1. **Competence** – Build skills, believe in your abilities
2. **Confidence** – Trust yourself based on past success
3. **Connection** – Strong relationships provide support
4. **Character** – Know your values, act with integrity
5. **Contribution** – Help others, find meaning
6. **Coping** – Healthy strategies for stress
7. **Control** – Focus on what you can influence

### Bouncing Back from Setbacks

**After a failure:**

1. **Feel it** – It's okay to be disappointed
2. **Reality check** – Is it as bad as it feels?
3. **Learn** – What specifically went wrong?
4. **Adjust** – What will you do differently?
5. **Move forward** – Take action, don't dwell

**Self-talk reframes:**

- "I failed" → "I learned what doesn't work"
- "I can't do this" → "I can't do this yet"
- "This is a disaster" → "This is a setback I can recover from"
